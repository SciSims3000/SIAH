Question Library 23 August 2026

Building a question library for thinking, not just remembering

I have been building a large curated question bank for Investigating Science and Working Scientifically. The aim is not simply to collect more questions. It is to make it easier for students to practise the kind of thinking that science examinations actually demand.

The library draws on Australian and international assessment sources. Students can filter the collection to find questions that match what they need to practise, while teachers can see richer metadata about syllabus links, skills, question type, context and difficulty.

The important part for me is transfer. Students should not only recognise knowledge in the form in which it was first taught; they should be able to apply it when the wording, data or context is unfamiliar. A broad, carefully tagged bank gives them repeated chances to make that leap.

There are support guides for students and teachers, plus a guide to the tabs and columns behind the library. The working sheets will stay in Google Drive while I test the filters, metadata and classroom workflow.

Astra/ChatGPT did much of the heavy lifting in harvesting, tagging and auditing the questions. I drove the pedagogical design: what belongs, what the tags need to reveal, and how the finished library should help students become more flexible scientific thinkers. That division of labour has turned a very large idea into something I can actually use.

Welcome 13 July 2026

Welcome to Science in a Hurry!

This started an eon ago as a way of making bespoke YouTube videos for my classes. During COVID-19, it became a way of creating revision materials.

Then came AI.

AI is many things, and in all honesty, the arguments for and against it contain plenty of valid points and positions. For me, however, it became a game-changer.

It allowed me to take all the little ideas that popped into my head and turn them into functioning resources.

It would be cool to have a set of haiku poems for every lesson. Done.

Create Learning Intentions and Success Criteria without cursing pedagogical constructivists. Done.

Make a simulation showing how diseases spread during a zombie apocalypse. Done.

Take an old extinction Flash game and make it live again. Done.

Make a quiz platform that sends student results to a Google Sheet so progress can be tracked. Nearly there.

I do not need to spend a fortune or hire a development team to create these resources. They are built around the way I like to teach: engage students first, and then take the learning deeper.

It does not always work, but when it does, it is magic.

Feel free to drop me a line if there is a resource you think might be useful. It may take me less time to build than you think.
